Answer: The procedure for constitutional amendment in India follows a set of detailed steps outlined in Article 54. This article provides the framework for amending the Constitution and specifies the conditions under which such amendments can be initiated. Here's a summary:

1. **Procedure for Constitutional Amendment**:
   - The process involves two stages: introduction by Parliament and presentation to the President.
   - Parliament may amend the Constitution only through bills introduced in both Houses of Parliament.

2. **Amendment by Bills**:
   - When a bill is passed by either House with at least a majority vote (50% or more), it must be presented to the President for approval.
   - The President can grant assent to the Bill if it passes through both Houses without amendments.
   - In some cases, amendments may seek to make changes in articles 162, 241, or Part V of Part VI, or any part of the List in Part XI.

3. **Limitations on Constitutional Amendment**:
   - Amendments made under Article 54 (including provisions of Part III) are not subject to judicial review.
   - The President can make adaptations and modifications to laws that have been passed by Parliament, including repeal or amendment, as long as they meet the constitutional requirements.

4. **Regulations**:
   - The Constitution provides for certain detailed procedures in Article 13 regarding amendments of this Constitution, which are considered amendments under the amended article.
   - These regulations may be subject to judicial review if deemed necessary or expedient by Parliament.

In summary, the procedure for constitutional amendment is outlined through a series of steps involving bills introduced by both Houses and their presentation to the President. The President has the authority to grant assent if the bill passes, and any modifications made are subject to judicial review.
